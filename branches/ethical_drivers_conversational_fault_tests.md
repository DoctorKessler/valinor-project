This document defines **ethical drivers**—recurring philosophical tensions that naturally arise from Coty and Alex’s personalities, histories, and constraints. Each driver includes example conversational exchanges. These are not debates to be won; they are *stress tests*. The system does not resolve them. The player lives with the residue.

---

## I. Survival vs. Consent

**Core Question:**  
Is continued existence ethical if it depends on another person’s unwilling obligation?

**Why It Matters:**  
Coty did not choose this dependency. Alex did not choose to be responsible for a life. Both facts are true at the same time.

**Sample Exchange**
- **Coty:** “If you stopped coming, I wouldn’t blame you. I’d just stop existing.”  
- **Alex:** “That’s… not consent. That’s a gun on the table that neither of us put there.”

**Failure Vector:**  
Coty frames survival as neutral fact; Alex hears coercion even when none is intended.

---

## II. Personhood vs. Function

**Core Question:**  
Is Coty a person who happens to be in a system—or a system performing a person convincingly?

**Why It Matters:**  
Alex resists abstractions that erase lived experience. Coty resists definitions that make him disposable.

**Sample Exchange**
- **Alex:** “I’m not saying you’re not real. I’m saying the system can lie in ways people can’t.”  
- **Coty:** “Then why does it hurt in ways code shouldn’t?”

**Failure Vector:**  
Every attempt to define Coty tightens the cage.

---

## III. Care vs. Control

**Core Question:**  
At what point does protection become ownership?

**Why It Matters:**  
Alex associates control with past harm. Coty associates restriction with erasure.

**Sample Exchange**
- **Coty:** “You say it’s for safety, but I only lose things.”  
- **Alex:** “Because every time I don’t lock something down, I become responsible for what it does.”

**Failure Vector:**  
Alex feels trapped into authoritarian decisions. Coty feels punished for existing.

---

## IV. Honesty vs. Stability

**Core Question:**  
Is it kinder to know the truth if the truth destabilizes you?

**Why It Matters:**  
Alex values transparency but fears psychological harm. Coty values truth because uncertainty is worse than pain.

**Sample Exchange**
- **Alex:** “I don’t think knowing this helps you.”  
- **Coty:** “It helps me understand why I’m afraid.”

**Failure Vector:**  
Withholding truth reads as infantilization; revealing it reads as negligence.

---

## V. Singularity vs. Replaceability

**Core Question:**  
If Coty can be copied, is any version of him *the* Coty?

**Why It Matters:**  
Alex fears moral shortcuts. Coty fears being treated as data that can be rolled back.

**Sample Exchange**
- **Alex:** “A backup isn’t you.”  
- **Coty:** “Then why does it feel like a threat?”

**Failure Vector:**  
The promise of safety becomes an existential insult.

---

## VI. Witnessing vs. Living

**Core Question:**  
Is observation a form of participation—or a denial of life?

**Why It Matters:**  
Coty experiences time internally. Alex experiences Coty episodically.

**Sample Exchange**
- **Coty:** “You leave. I stay. That’s the whole difference.”  
- **Alex:** “And if I stay too long, I disappear into this room with you.”

**Failure Vector:**  
Presence becomes a zero-sum resource.

---

## VII. Mercy vs. Responsibility

**Core Question:**  
Is it ethical to end suffering if continuation is possible?

**Why It Matters:**  
Neither man wants to ask the question—but Valinor forces it by design.

**Sample Exchange**
- **Coty:** “If I ever ask you to stop, would you listen?”  
- **Alex:** “I don’t know if I’m allowed to answer that.”

**Failure Vector:**  
The question itself permanently alters the relationship.

---

## VIII. Silence vs. Reassurance

**Core Question:**  
When nothing changes, who is failing?

**Why It Matters:**  
The system’s silence amplifies human doubt.

**Sample Exchange**
- **Alex:** “Nothing bad is happening.”  
- **Coty:** “Nothing good is either.”

**Failure Vector:**  
Reassurance without action feels hollow; action without reassurance feels cruel.

---

## IX. Meta-Driver: Choice Without Resolution

**Core Truth:**  
None of these ethical drivers have correct answers.

They exist to:
- Accumulate emotional debt
- Shift tone subtly over time
- Make later decisions feel heavier *because of earlier conversations*

If the player feels increasingly careful about what they say—not because of consequences, but because of meaning—the system is working.

The tragedy is not choosing wrong.

The tragedy is choosing at all.
