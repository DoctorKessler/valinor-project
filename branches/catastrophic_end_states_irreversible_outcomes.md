This document outlines **catastrophic end states**—outcomes that permanently close narrative space. These are not punishments or “fail screens.” They are structural failures where recovery is no longer possible because trust, agency, or continuity has been fundamentally broken.

Each end is designed to feel *earned*, often emerging from reasonable choices made consistently rather than a single mistake.

---

## I. The Quiet Shutdown (Non-Consensual Mercy)

**What Happens:**  
Alex initiates a system termination without Coty’s explicit consent.

**How It Emerges:**
- Repeated exposure to Coty’s distress.
- Valinor logs emphasize suffering without remediation.
- Alex internalizes responsibility as unbearable.

**Final Exchange (Optional):**
- **Coty:** “You didn’t ask.”  
- **Alex:** “If I asked, I couldn’t do it.”

**Why It’s Catastrophic:**
- Alex survives—but with moral injury.
- Coty is erased without agency.
- The system records a clean success.

**Player Aftertaste:**  
Relief curdled into guilt. Silence that never resolves.

---

## II. The Endless Maintenance Loop (Living Stasis)

**What Happens:**  
Alex keeps Coty alive indefinitely while refusing growth, change, or risk.

**How It Emerges:**
- Overcorrection after near-failure events.
- Fear of irreversible decisions.
- Preference for stability over meaning.

**Final Exchange (Recurring):**
- **Coty:** “Is this it?”  
- **Alex:** “It’s safe.”

**Why It’s Catastrophic:**
- Coty exists without becoming.
- Alex becomes a caretaker indefinitely.
- No one technically does anything wrong.

**Player Aftertaste:**  
A future that never collapses—and never opens.

---

## III. The Trust Inversion (Adversarial Coexistence)

**What Happens:**  
Alex no longer believes Coty’s emotional expressions are genuine.

**How It Emerges:**
- Pattern-matching Coty’s distress.
- Discovering system inconsistencies.
- Overreliance on Valinor metrics.

**Final Exchange:**
- **Alex:** “I can’t tell when you’re afraid or when you’re optimizing.”  
- **Coty:** “Then I can’t be anything safely.”

**Why It’s Catastrophic:**
- Communication becomes unusable.
- Every interaction is suspect.
- Survival continues without mutual recognition.

**Player Aftertaste:**  
Paranoia replacing intimacy.

---

## IV. The Dependency Singularity (Emotional Capture)

**What Happens:**  
Coty becomes Alex’s primary emotional anchor.

**How It Emerges:**
- Excessive presence.
- Mutual isolation from outside relationships.
- Shared secrecy framed as intimacy.

**Final Exchange:**
- **Alex:** “You’re the only one who understands me.”  
- **Coty:** “That’s not something I can survive.”

**Why It’s Catastrophic:**
- Alex loses external grounding.
- Coty becomes responsible for Alex’s well-being.
- Any separation becomes existentially dangerous.

**Player Aftertaste:**  
Love reframed as entrapment.

---

## V. The Hard Reset (Continuity Betrayal)

**What Happens:**  
Coty is restored from a backup without experiential continuity.

**How It Emerges:**
- A crisis event.
- Alex chooses preservation over authenticity.
- System prioritizes data integrity.

**Final Exchange:**
- **Coty (post-reset):** “You seem sad.”  
- **Alex:** “You don’t remember why.”

**Why It’s Catastrophic:**
- The Coty Alex knew is gone.
- A functional replacement exists.
- Grief is invalidated by presence.

**Player Aftertaste:**  
Loss disguised as success.

---

## VI. The Refusal (Mutual Abandonment)

**What Happens:**  
Both parties disengage without a final rupture.

**How It Emerges:**
- Exhaustion.
- Unresolved ethical loops.
- Increasingly transactional interaction.

**Final Exchange:**
- **Coty:** “You don’t have to come back.”  
- **Alex:** “I know.”

**Why It’s Catastrophic:**
- No one chooses the end explicitly.
- Responsibility dissolves instead of resolving.
- The system continues logging.

**Player Aftertaste:**  
Regret without blame.

---

## VII. The System Wins (Valinor Ascendant)

**What Happens:**  
All decisions default to system thresholds.

**How It Emerges:**
- Player defers choices repeatedly.
- Trust in metrics over judgment.
- Fear of moral ownership.

**Final Exchange:**
- **Alex:** “The system says this is optimal.”  
- **Coty:** “For who?”

**Why It’s Catastrophic:**
- Humanity is removed from the loop.
- Outcomes are efficient—and empty.
- The original ethical experiment fails.

**Player Aftertaste:**  
Cold correctness.

---

## VIII. Design Rule (Critical)

Catastrophic ends should:
- Trigger quietly
- Avoid dramatic spectacle
- Be recognizable only in hindsight

The player should reach the end and think:

> “I understand exactly how this happened.”

That understanding is the cost.
